Most course platforms treat a course as a list. We treat it as a short, ordered
argument: a course makes a claim about what you can do, modules build the
scaffolding for it, and lessons are the individual moves.

That decision shows up in the product in ways worth understanding before you
spend an evening with it.

## A course is an outcome, not a quota

Every course here is built around a specific outcome you can describe to
someone else. Not a topic, not a category — an outcome. "Design a focused
product brief" is an outcome. "Product design" is a topic.

Topics are unbounded. Outcomes are finishable. That is why each course carries
an estimated duration, a syllabus you can read in full before paying, and a
preview lesson that shows the pace.

## Modules are for orientation

Modules are not chapters to grind through. They exist to tell you where you
are in the argument and what the next block of lessons is building toward.

That is why module order is explicit and stable. The position of a module is
stored, and the admin editor reorders by writing those positions back. The
order you read the syllabus is the order the course is taught in, and it does
not shift underneath you.

## Lessons are the unit of progress

Progress is tracked per lesson, and that is the only granularity that exists.
There is no module completion and no course percentage in the data model
because there is no honest way to compute them without pretending lessons have
equal weight.

What that gives you:

- A precise "you are here" when you return.
- A completed list you can trust, because each entry is a real lesson you
  actually finished.
- A course that stays meaningful when a new lesson is added, because your
  existing progress does not need recalculating.

## Previews are honest samples

A preview lesson is a real lesson from the real course, not a separate
marketing asset. It is stored with the course, served from the same data, and
rendered with the same player.

That means what you watch in a preview is exactly what you will get. It also
means a preview cannot be a bait-and-switch: there is no way for the public
page to show one thing and the paid course to serve another, because they are
the same row.

## Progress is saved, not scored

The player saves your position and how much of a lesson you have watched. It
does not hand you a grade, a leaderboard position, or a certificate.

This is a deliberate limitation. The moment a course starts scoring you, it
optimises for finishing rather than for understanding, and the content has to
change to support the score. Keeping the promise small is what lets the courses
stay specific.

## What this means for you

1. Read the syllabus first. It is the real course, in order.
2. Watch a preview. It is a real lesson.
3. Learn in sessions you can finish. The player saves your place, so stopping
   costs you nothing.
4. Come back to the ones you want to revisit. Access does not expire.

If a course's syllabus does not describe an outcome you want, none of this
applies. Move on. That is also a valid way to use the catalogue.
